\title{When Graph Structure Becomes a Liability: \\
  Temporal Distribution Shift Degrades GNN Fraud Detection \\
  but Graph-Derived Representations Survive in Hybrid Ensembles}

\author{Saket Maganti}

\begin{abstract}
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are widely applied to transaction fraud
detection, yet published evaluations on the Elliptic Bitcoin Dataset
use transductive protocols that expose test-time graph structure during
training.  We present a comprehensive benchmark of GCN, GraphSAGE,
GAT, and MLP under strict inductive evaluation with multi-seed
validation and per-timestep reporting across 15 test periods.

Our evaluation reveals three findings with direct practical
implications.  First, a plain MLP (F1 = 0.744) outperforms every
tested GNN (best: GraphSAGE, F1 = 0.697), and Random Forest achieves
F1 = 0.820 with no graph structure.  Second, a multi-seed
graph-structure ablation demonstrates that the Bitcoin transaction
graph actively degrades performance: shuffling edges randomly improves
F1 from 0.268 to 0.383.  Third, a concatenation hybrid that combines
GNN embeddings with raw tabular features through a Random Forest
classifier achieves F1 = 0.807, recovering nearly all performance and
showing that graph-learned representations carry complementary signal
when properly fused.

The performance collapse is driven by temporal distribution shift: the
fraud rate drops 39$\times$ between training and the most adverse test
steps.  Temperature scaling and threshold tuning yield $\Delta$F1
$\approx$ 0, confirming the collapse is structural.  Business cost
analysis under asymmetric penalties shows that the model with the best
F1 is not always the cheapest to operate.

These results have direct implications for fraud detection system
design: production systems should retrain regularly, monitor for
distributional drift, consider hybrid architectures that decouple
representation learning from classification, and evaluate on
operational cost functions rather than symmetric metrics.
\end{abstract}
