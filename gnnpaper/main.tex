\title{When Graph Structure Becomes a Liability:\\
  A Critical Re-Evaluation of Graph Neural Networks for\\
  Bitcoin Fraud Detection under Temporal Distribution Shift}

\author{Saket Maganti}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are the dominant architecture for fraud
detection on transaction graphs, and published results on the Elliptic
Bitcoin Dataset consistently place GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, and EvolveGCN
above feature-only baselines.  We show this ranking is fragile under
two protocol changes that remove evaluation leakage.  First, every
prior Elliptic study uses \emph{transductive} training, in which
message passing sees test-period edges, and reports a single aggregate
metric that masks a near-total collapse after step~42.  Second, several
widely cited ``inductive'' setups in fact train the encoder on the full
graph and only mask the loss---a protocol that still leaks test-period
features through batch statistics and aggregation.

Under a strictly inductive protocol (encoder trained on the
time-step\,$\le$\,34 relabeled subgraph, evaluated on the full graph
at inference) with 10 seeds per configuration and per-timestep
reporting, Random Forest on raw features reaches F1\,=\,0.821 and
beats every GNN and every hybrid that uses graph-derived
representations.  GraphSAGE, the strongest graph encoder under this
protocol, reaches F1\,=\,0.689\,$\pm$\,0.017 (10 seeds); the 3-layer MLP reaches
F1\,=\,0.549\,$\pm$\,0.015, \emph{reversing} the MLP$>$GNN ranking
reported by evaluations that use random-partition mini-batching.  A paired controlled experiment
confirms the mechanism: across 10 seeds GraphSAGE scores
F1\,=\,0.294\,$\pm$\,0.028 when trained transductively and
F1\,=\,0.689\,$\pm$\,0.017 when trained on the inductive subgraph, a
39.5-point paired gap (Cohen's $d$\,=\,15.8, $p$\,=\,2.6$\times$10$^{-12}$)
driven purely by exposure to test-period adjacency during training.

The paper's original constructive claim---that a concatenation hybrid
of GNN embeddings and raw features reaches F1\,=\,0.807---does not
survive the clean protocol: across 10 seeds the same hybrid falls to
F1\,=\,0.699\,$\pm$\,0.015, and a paired ablation that swaps the GNN
encoder for an MLP of identical capacity shows graph structure
contributes a statistically reliable but small lift
($+0.018$ F1, Welch $p = 0.015$, $d = +1.20$), dwarfed by the
$0.124$ F1 gap to raw features alone; the 0.807 number was a
transductive leakage artefact, and raw features remain the strongest
input.  Temperature scaling
yields $\Delta$F1\,$\approx$\,0, ruling out calibration as a remedy.
A multi-seed edge-shuffle ablation further shows that on Elliptic,
\emph{randomly shuffled edges outperform the real transaction graph}:
under this dataset's sparse, prior-shifted conditions, the topology
is not merely uninformative, it is actively harmful.  We audit the
most-cited prior Elliptic GNN results and the classical baselines
reported alongside them, and find that every one relies on either
transductive training or evaluation-time shortcuts that prevent an
apples-to-apples comparison with our numbers.  Code,
checkpoints, and the strict-inductive protocol are released so that
future work can be compared against a leakage-free baseline.
\end{abstract}
